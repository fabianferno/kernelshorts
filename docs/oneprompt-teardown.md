# OnePrompt teardown — what @semedyarov's reels are made of

Sources: the 4 free guide pages (`reference/guide/page1-4.png`), 6 downloaded
reels (`reference/semedyarov/*.mp4`), local whisper transcripts
(`reference/semedyarov/transcripts/*.vtt`), and contact sheets at 1 frame / 4 s
(`reference/semedyarov/frames/*-sheet.jpg`). `reference/` is gitignored.

## 1. The system (from the guide)

- Product: "Build your own AI video editor" — a PDF guide + "studio kit":
  **23 prompts, 4 Claude skills**, CLAUDE.md, `brand/style.md`, `brand/voice.md`,
  `prompts.md`, a `References/` folder.
- Entry point is a **`/reel <topic>` skill**. Prompt 09 opens with: *"Make a reel
  about [TOPIC]. Follow CLAUDE.md, brand/style.md and brand/voice.md. Work in
  this order and stop at the three gates:"*
- **Three human gates:**
  1. **Script** — research, fact-check, **8 beats, a visual for every line**.
  2. **First 10 s** — voice, timings, scene plan, then *only the hook* rendered.
  3. **Final cut** — all scenes, captions, host, sound, speed-up, master, check.
- **Host = pose library**: ~25 still cartoon drawings of himself that **swap on
  trigger words** ("First" → point up, "Third" → three fingers, "So why?" →
  shrug, "Not" → stop palm, "Approved" → thumbs up, "Follow me" → wave) plus a
  **mouth that moves with the voice**. Drawn once with an image generator from
  his photos (prompt 13).
- The 23 prompts map the whole workflow: studio setup → first render (no voice,
  no API key) → **learn a style from 3–8 reference videos** → who-you-are →
  ideas backlog → research+script (gate 1) → **hook lab** → voice → one-line run
  → **10-second test** (gate 2) → **make it 3D** → word-synced captions →
  cartoon host → make the host act → sound & master → **fix one moment** (plain
  words) → approve & learn (gate 3) → rough-cut own footage / graphics on
  footage (route B) → multi-platform export → final check → hand-off to new chat
  → **learn from your numbers** (insights feed back in).
- Everything is code-generated by Claude; 3D is real-time rendered (looks like
  three.js / WebGL scenes with soft studio lighting), not stock.

## 2. Format (measured)

| | |
|---|---|
| Canvas | 1080×1920, 30 fps, AAC 44.1 kHz |
| Length | **100–127 s** (all six) — not a 30–60 s short |
| Hard cuts | only 4–13 per reel (avg shot 8–20 s) — scenes **flow** via camera moves and object morphs inside one 3D world rather than cutting |
| Visual change | something new every ~2–4 s (new object, number, card, pose) |

## 3. Frame anatomy (every frame, same layout)

Top → bottom:

1. **Chrome bar** (after the hook): logo `OnePrompt.ch` left · a pill with the
   **chapter name** (`THE SUSPECT`, `THE TEST`, `THE TRAP`, `THE FIX`,
   `THE HEAT`, `THE CATCH`, `YOUR PART`) and segmented progress dashes · a blue
   `?` badge right. The chapter pill is a "where am I" device that keeps people
   watching to the next chapter.
2. **Word caption** near the top (~y 10%), 1–3 words at a time, Inter-ish
   sans: context words light grey, **the stressed word bold white/black inside
   a selection-box highlight** (the same "Figma selection rectangle with
   handles" motif as the guide's headlines). Numbers get the box.
3. **Main stage** (middle 55%): one hero 3D object on a soft studio floor, OR a
   UI card (receipt-style source card, ChatGPT chat bubble, report-card,
   calendar of dates, bar meters, a Science-paper header). Monospace
   **micro-labels** in pill tags (`THE TROLLEY PROBLEM`, `2 CATS`,
   `QUARTZ = 10× MORE ACCURATE`) annotate objects.
4. **Big counter numbers** (`1,700 W/cm²`, `32,768 → 512 Hz`, `2,000`, `34%`)
   that tick up — huge, tabular, with a mono subtitle.
5. **Tiny source footnote** bottom-left in grey (publication + date) — credibility
   without slowing the read.
6. **Cartoon host**, waist-up, bottom-centre, ~25% of height, always present,
   pose changes on keywords.

Backgrounds alternate **dark (#111, spotlight cone)** and **light (#f2f2f2 with
a faint grid)** per chapter, which also acts as a soft chapter break.

Recurring 3D props: a white **robot** stands in for "the AI"; cartoon cows/cats
on a trolley track; chips, watches, gears, tuning fork, trophy, crowds of tiny
figures, Switzerland map with extruded bars; a **phone mock** showing the reel
itself + comments for the CTA.

## 4. Script formula (from transcripts)

Every reel follows the same 8-ish beat arc:

1. **Hook (0–5 s)** — "Did you know that…" + a counter-intuitive, concrete fact
   with a name brand and a number. ("Microsoft carved tiny rivers into an AI
   chip.") 5 of 6 open with "Did you know"; the other opens with a quote.
2. **The question (5–10 s)** — restate as a *why*: "So why would anyone run
   water through a computer chip?" This is the open loop.
3. **The reframe** — short punchy answer that is itself a riddle: "Because AI
   doesn't just think. It sweats." / "It isn't a chip story. It's a coal story."
4. **Personal stake (1 line)** — "I came from Kazakhstan to do my masters at
   ETH…", "I'm from there, so I checked." Earns authority and is what the
   avatar is for.
5. **Explain via "Imagine…"** — a physical analogy you can draw in 3D
   ("Imagine a hairdryer that never switches off on silicon smaller than your
   palm", "Imagine a student graded on whether you smiled").
6. **Numbered structure** — "three parts: First… Second… Third…" (drives the
   pose library: point-up, two, three).
7. **Twist** — "But here's the catch." / "And here's the plot twist." Second
   open loop mid-video to stop the drop-off at ~60 s.
8. **"That's also why…" → actionable takeaway for the viewer** ("Don't use
   thinking mode to fix a typo", "start its answer with 'Wait a minute'").
9. **Callback close** — repeat the hook fact with the new meaning ("So yes,
   Apple sells twice as many watches. But…").
10. **CTA** — "Follow me and send this to the friend who…" (share bait) or
    "Comment 'one prompt' and I'll send you the link" (lead magnet).

Voice: short sentences (often 3–6 words), present tense, every claim has a
specific number + named source + year. Niche: **AI × Switzerland × his own
story** — narrow and personal.

## 5. Why it holds attention

- Open loops stacked: hook question → "three parts" → "but here's the catch" →
  "three words that make it push back" (promised early, paid at the end).
- Something visibly changes every 2–4 s, but the *world* persists — no jarring
  cuts, so it feels premium rather than frantic.
- Numbers are animated, not spoken-only; every abstract idea gets a physical
  object.
- The host's poses punctuate the speech like a presenter, adding a human
  without filming.
- Chapter pill + progress dashes = visible progress.

## 5b. How the 3D is made (motion evidence)

3-second frame strips (2 fps) of five props show **real-time 3D written as
code, not generated assets or stills**:

- **Built from primitives, toy/clay style:** the cows are boxes + spheres, the
  cats are stacked capsules, the toaster is a rounded box with cylinder knobs,
  the watch is a cylinder case with rounded-box straps. That is the signature
  of an LLM writing three.js geometry, not of image-to-3D or stock models.
- **Parametric animation:** the watch hands move, the tuning-fork counter ticks
  16,716 → 32,768 in sync with glow rings, and the chip's heat-map cells
  re-texture into blue "rivers" in place.
- **Instancing:** "100 investors" is ~100 copies of one figure mesh fanned
  into an arc.
- **One lighting rig everywhere:** soft studio key + contact shadow on a seamless
  floor, flipped between a light grid sweep and a dark spotlight cone.
- The camera dollies inside the scene rather than cutting.
- Props recur across reels (the white robot = "the AI"), which points to a
  growing prop library plus a shared style file, not one-offs.

Likely stack: Claude Code skills that write a per-beat HTML/React composition
(Remotion + three.js / react-three-fiber, or equivalent), with UI cards in
HTML/CSS, pose-PNG host, TTS word timestamps for captions, rendered
headlessly; "render only the first 10 s" = render a frame range. HyperFrames
plus three.js covers the same ground for us, as long as every scene is driven
by the composition clock (seekable, deterministic), not by free-running
requestAnimationFrame.
